# The Diligent Shadow, for Roblox

An experimental port of the web version (`public/games/thedilligentshadow/`) to Roblox. It uses the same models (the Quaternius woman, the Poly Haven chair, the WebXR hand) and the same lathe-turned switch, and ports her behaviour, gait, kick, push, sit, stomp and turning from `exhibit.js`.

Open `TheDiligentShadow.rbxlx` in Roblox Studio and press Play.

- **C** (or **Enter**, or a click on the plinth): the close-up (the web version's close view) and back.
- **E** (near the switch, or in the close-up): hold the switch on. **F**: flip it off.
- In the close-up, drag the switch with the mouse, as on the web.
- **On a phone or tablet:** tap the plinth for the close-up (and again to step back); there, drag the switch on with a finger and keep it there to hold it. The thumbstick and jump button hide in the close-up. Phones can be held either way: in portrait the close-up takes the web version's taller view, and the text wraps and sits above the touch controls.
- The controls are listed along the bottom of the screen, for keys or for touch, whichever was used last.

## How it looks the same on every graphics level

Roblox's lighting changes completely between graphics levels. On the lowest, the lamp's pool smears across the whole wall and real shadows disappear. So nothing important depends on it:

- **Shadows** are flat silhouettes on the wall. Every frame each model is skinned on the CPU and projected through the lamp into an `EditableMesh`. They are unlit (Neon) in exactly the colour of the wall where no lamp light reaches. So they are always drawn: they emerge as the pool brightens round them and vanish into the dark as it fades, as real shadows do.
- **The pool of light** is an unlit image on the wall (`Environment.luau`), computed from the web version's spotlight. **The plinth's faces** are unlit skins, and the floor is unlit, with the downlight's glow round the plinth's foot as an unlit disc of vertex colours (carried in the switch body's mesh).
- **The switch's steel** (`Switch.luau`) is shaded per vertex every frame. It reflects the camera ray into an environment like the web version's (dark room, the pool ahead, the downlight above, the lamp behind you, the white plinth below).
- **The visitor's hand** is lit per vertex from the downlight and the lamp. The hand and its shadow fade out at the wrist with a 4×4 Bayer dither. The dither is geometry: the band's triangles are cut into small pieces, kept or dropped against the fade.

This was tested at graphics levels 1, 7, 14 and 21 in the close-up and the room view. Only the visitor's own Roblox avatar is still lit by Roblox.

## Files

- `src/client/DiligentShadow.client.luau`: everything the player sees and does (LocalScript; each player has their own shadow)
- `src/shared/Environment.luau`, `src/shared/Switch.luau`, `src/shared/Geometry.luau`: the painted room, the switch, the exhibit's dimensions (metres, at 3 studs a metre)
- `src/server/BuildGallery.server.luau`: builds the room, plinth and lights when the server starts
- `src/assets/*.luau`: model data, generated by `tools/prepare_assets.py` from the web version's assets

Rebuild the place after editing:

```sh
python3 tools/prepare_assets.py   # only when the models change
python3 tools/build_place.py
```

Runtime meshes are limited (the place uses six: the wall's shadows, the hand's dithered shadow band, the hand and its dithered band, the switch body and its lever), so new geometry should join an existing mesh. `EditableMesh` and `EditableImage` work in Studio. To publish, Roblox requires the publishing account to be ID-verified.

## Not ported yet

The hair and cloak cloth (her ponytail is the model's own, rigid), her finger grip, sounds, the orbit controls in the close-up, and sharing one switch between players.
